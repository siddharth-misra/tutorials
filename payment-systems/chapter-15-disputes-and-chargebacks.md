# 15: Disputes and Chargebacks

A payment is not fully complete just because it was authorized and settled. Cardholders can question transactions later, merchants can defend them, and the network rules determine how evidence, liability, and timelines are handled.

Disputes and chargebacks matter because they translate customer trust problems into operational and financial workflows across issuers, acquirers, merchants, and the network. This chapter explains the end-to-end dispute process so you can understand why clean data, strong fulfillment evidence, and precise reason codes matter.

---

## 15.1 Why Disputes Exist

Disputes protect consumers and system trust when transactions are unauthorized, defective, or unclear.

Chargebacks are a formal process, not only a refund event.

---

## 15.2 Typical Chargeback Flow

1. Cardholder raises dispute with issuer.
2. Issuer evaluates and may initiate chargeback.
3. Acquirer notifies merchant.
4. Merchant may accept or provide evidence (representment).
5. Further arbitration may occur under network rules.

---

## 15.3 Reason Codes

Reason codes classify dispute type, such as:

- fraud/unauthorized,
- processing errors,
- goods/services issues.

Correct coding drives workflow and evidence requirements.

---

## 15.4 Evidence and Representment

Strong evidence may include:

- order confirmation,
- delivery proof,
- usage logs,
- communication history,
- authentication data.

Evidence quality materially affects recovery outcomes.

---

## 15.5 Roles and Responsibilities

- **Merchant:** Proper fulfillment, records, and representment readiness.
- **Acquirer/processor:** Workflow support and merchant guidance.
- **Issuer:** Cardholder protection and rule-based dispute handling.
- **VISA/network:** Standards, timelines, and arbitration framework.

---

## 15.6 Operational Best Practices

1. Monitor dispute ratio by segment.
2. Fix root causes (fraud, UX confusion, fulfillment issues).
3. Maintain evidence retention policies.
4. Use reason-code analytics for prevention.

---

## 15.7 Common Mistakes

1. Treating disputes as only back-office work.
2. Inconsistent evidence quality.
3. Missing deadlines in dispute lifecycle.

---

## 15.8 Glossary

- **Chargeback:** Forced transaction reversal through scheme rules.
- **Representment:** Merchant/acquirer response contesting chargeback.
- **Arbitration:** Final rule-based adjudication stage.
- **Reason code:** Standardized dispute classification.
