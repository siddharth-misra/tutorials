# 28: Squad Leadership and Execution

Strong payment teams do not deliver reliably by accident. They need clear ownership, disciplined prioritization, effective decision-making, and engineering habits that keep delivery moving without sacrificing system safety.

This chapter explains squad leadership as day-to-day execution work rather than as management rhetoric. It focuses on how leaders convert business goals into healthy delivery rhythms, manage trade-offs, and keep quality visible while complexity increases.

---

## 28.1 Leadership in Regulated Engineering

Engineering leaders in payments balance:

- delivery speed,
- reliability,
- risk/compliance,
- people growth.

---

## 28.2 Priority Management

Use explicit prioritization across:

- customer-impact features,
- stability/reliability work,
- compliance deadlines,
- technical debt reduction.

Visible trade-offs build trust.

---

## 28.3 Hands-On Leadership

High-impact leaders contribute through:

- architecture reviews,
- code review quality,
- incident coaching,
- design simplification.

Hands-on does not mean micromanagement.

---

## 28.4 Cross-Functional Collaboration

Payments require close alignment with:

- product,
- operations,
- risk,
- compliance,
- support teams.

Misalignment here causes avoidable rework.

---

## 28.5 Team Health and Culture

Strong squads invest in:

- psychological safety,
- clear feedback loops,
- growth plans,
- sustainable on-call practices.

Healthy teams ship better systems.

---

## 28.6 Common Mistakes

1. Feature-only planning without reliability budget.
2. Hero culture instead of repeatable process.
3. No mentoring pipeline for future leads.

---

## 28.7 Glossary

- **Technical debt:** Future cost caused by short-term design shortcuts.
- **Psychological safety:** Team environment where people can raise concerns safely.
- **On-call:** Rotation responsible for production incidents.
