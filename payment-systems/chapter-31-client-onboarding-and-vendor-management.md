# 31: Client Onboarding and Vendor Management

In payment businesses, signing a client or selecting a vendor is only the start of the work. The real challenge is turning agreements into reliable operations through readiness checks, integration planning, compliance coordination, and ongoing governance.

This chapter explains onboarding and vendor management as execution disciplines with direct impact on revenue, support load, and risk. It shows how structured handoffs and operating cadences prevent avoidable failures after contracts are signed.

---

## 31.1 Onboarding as a Product

Client onboarding should be designed like a product journey, not an ad-hoc project.

Key outcomes:

- faster time to first transaction,
- lower launch risk,
- clearer stakeholder accountability.

---

## 31.2 Typical Onboarding Stages

1. Discovery and scope definition
2. Compliance and risk review
3. Technical integration and certification
4. Pilot/go-live readiness
5. Hypercare and transition to steady-state

---

## 31.3 Integration Readiness Checklist

- API contract alignment,
- test environment completion,
- operational runbooks,
- reconciliation/reporting validation,
- incident communication paths.

---

## 31.4 Vendor and Partner Governance

Good governance includes:

- SLA monitoring,
- periodic risk reviews,
- security attestations,
- incident escalation protocols,
- performance and roadmap reviews.

---

## 31.5 Stakeholder Communication

Strong communication model defines:

- executive sponsors,
- delivery leads,
- weekly status and risk reporting,
- change-control decisions.

---

## 31.6 Common Mistakes

1. Launching before reconciliation and support readiness.
2. No ownership map across client and vendor teams.
3. Weak post-launch stabilization process.

---

## 31.7 Glossary

- **Hypercare:** Intensified support period right after launch.
- **Readiness gate:** Formal checkpoint before advancing lifecycle stage.
- **SLA:** Service Level Agreement.

## 31.9 Recap

- Onboarding quality strongly influences long-term client success.
- Structured readiness gates reduce go-live failures.
- Vendor governance is continuous, not one-time due diligence.
